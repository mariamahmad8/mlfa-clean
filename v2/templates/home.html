How the hub works

The MLFA Email Hub watches your info@ inbox, uses GPT to sort each incoming email into a category, then either handles it automatically or hands it to a person to review.

Everything is configurable. You control which categories exist, what happens when an email matches each one, who gets forwarded what, and what auto-reply gets sent. The hub is designed so that a non-technical staff member can adjust behavior without touching code.

The three pieces

  1. The worker — a background program that polls Microsoft every ~30 seconds for new emails. You never see it.
  2. The Hub — this website. Staff review emails, edit settings, view audit history.
  3. The classifier — GPT reads each new email and decides which category it belongs to.

The life of an email

Every incoming email follows this path:

  1. Arrives at info@mlfa.org in Outlook.
  2. Worker picks it up within ~30 seconds on the next poll.
  3. Classifier reads it — GPT decides which category (donor, legal, jail_mail, volunteer, etc.).
  4. Router builds a plan — based on the category's rules: which folder to move to, who to forward to, what auto-reply to send, what tag to apply.
  5. Automation mode splits the path:
    • Automation on → plan executes immediately. Email gets forwarded, replied to, tagged, and moved.
    • Automation off → email lands in the review queue in the Hub. A staff member approves, rejects, or dismisses it.
  6. Outlook tag applied — PAIRActioned/<category> so the worker doesn't re-process it.
  7. Audit log entry written — records what happened, who did it, when.
ImportantOnce tagged with PAIRActioned, the email is never touched again by the worker. That's how we avoid double-processing.

Roles & permissions

Every user has one of three roles.

RoleCan doCannot do
Admin Everything. All inboxes, all settings, user management, category rules, audit logs, automation toggle. Nothing is restricted.
Owner Access the Hub + Settings for their assigned inboxes only. Can edit categories, recipients, templates, and toggle automation on inboxes they own. Cannot add/remove inboxes, manage users, or see inboxes they aren't assigned to.
Reviewer Only the review queue in the Hub, for their assigned inboxes. Can approve/reject/dismiss queued emails. No settings access at all. No inbox management. Can't toggle automation.

Review queue (the Hub)

This is where a person reviews emails the AI wasn't sure about. If automation is off for an inbox, every incoming email lands here.

Layout

  • Left panel — list of pending emails. Newest at top. Each row shows sender name, subject snippet, category tag.
  • Right panel — details of the selected email: from, category, amount detected, recipients, reason, and the full body.
  • Top bar — inbox tabs (if you can access more than one), automation toggle, settings gear, user menu.

Actions on the queue list

  • Click any row to open the email in the right panel.
  • Hover a row to see the small × button — dismisses it directly without opening.

Reviewing an email

Once you've opened an email in the right panel, you have three actions.

ButtonWhat it does
ApproveExecutes the AI's plan. Sends the reply, forwards to the recipients, tags with PAIRActioned/<category>, moves to the destination folder. The email disappears from the queue.
RejectMoves the email to Trash. You'll be asked for a reason (required). The reason gets logged for future improvement of the classifier. Nothing else happens.
DismissSilently drops the email — marks read, no forward, no reply. Used for spam / off-topic emails you don't want to trash but also don't want to act on.
Approve all pendingApproves every email currently in the queue. Use with caution.

Details shown per email

  • From — the sender's name (or "Unknown" if the AI couldn't identify it).
  • Category — the labels GPT applied. Multiple possible.
  • Amount detected — a dollar figure GPT extracted, if any. Used for donor/invoice threshold rules.
  • Recipients — who this email would be forwarded to if you approve.
  • Needs personal reply — GPT's judgment call: does this email need a real human response rather than a form reply? If yes, and the category has a "personal reply" template, that gets used instead of the default.
  • Reason — GPT's plain-English justification for its classification. "Sender mentions donation of $500 via PayPal" etc.
TipAfter you Approve, Reject, or Dismiss, the queue advances to the next email automatically. If there's no next one, you see "Select an email to review."

The Automation toggle

The switch at the top of the Hub. Two states:

  • Off — every email waits in the queue for a person. Safer, slower.
  • On — the AI processes emails immediately without human review. Fast, but you trust the classifier fully.
Important — automation always skips unknownsEven with automation ON, an email GPT can't confidently classify (empty categories, or a category with no matching rule) still gets sent to the review queue as a safety guardrail. Automation makes the known cases faster; it doesn't blindly auto-process everything.

Admins and owners can toggle this. Reviewers cannot.

Settings → Inboxes

The mailboxes the hub watches. Adding an inbox here means the worker will start polling it on the next cycle.

The table

  • Display name — what staff see. Editable.
  • Email — the actual mailbox address (e.g. info@mlfa.org).
  • Automation — Auto badge means automation is on, Manual means off.
  • Blocked senders — count of senders currently on the block list.
  • Actions — Stats, Edit, and (admin only) Delete.

Adding or editing an inbox

Clicking Add or Edit opens a modal with sections:

Basics

  • Email address — the mailbox to poll. Must exist in MLFA's Microsoft tenant.
  • Display name — human label shown throughout the UI.

Automation

  • Automatically process emails — the same toggle as the one on the Hub. When on, emails skip the review queue.
  • Use conversation history — the AI reads earlier messages in the same thread when classifying. Recommended for follow-ups. If off, GPT only sees the current message.
  • Import existing email: Last N days — how many days back to scan on the very first connection. Only used on initial setup. After that, delta sync handles new mail.

Filters

  • Ignore senders — one address per line. Emails from these addresses are silently skipped by the worker.
  • Internal email domains — one domain per line (e.g. mlfa.org). Emails from these domains are treated as internal/staff. Used by the "Skip if sender is internal" toggle on categories, and by the internal reply bridge.

Advanced (collapsed)

  • Internal reply bridge — an advanced routing feature. When enabled, staff can reply to a forwarded email with an [EXTERNAL] prefix and the system relays their reply back to the original sender. Leave off unless MLFA staff use this workflow.
  • AI behavior — the raw GPT prompt sent for every email in this inbox. Advanced. Editable but risky — this is what the classifier "thinks" is its job.

Stats

The Stats button on each inbox row opens a dashboard showing: emails handled today vs total, rejected/dismissed counts, average processing time, and estimated time saved vs manual work. Includes a 14-day chart of emails handled per day.

Settings → Categories

Category rules define what GPT can classify emails as, and what happens when it does. Everything the hub does routes through categories.

The list

  • Priority number — 1 is highest. When multiple categories match, the highest-priority one wins for actions like folder-move and reply.
  • Drag handle — grab the row to reorder priorities.
  • Label — the human-readable name. Editable.
  • Key — the permanent internal identifier GPT uses. Locked after creation.
  • Destination folder — where matching emails get moved. Empty = don't move.
  • Forward count — how many recipients this category forwards to.
  • Status — Active or Inactive.

Editing a category

Opens a modal with:

Header

  • Display name — big anchor at top. Category ID is shown as small metadata below (locked once created).
  • Active toggle — top-right. When off, GPT stops applying this category label until you turn it back on.

General

  • Display name — same as header, editable inline.
  • Move to folder — Outlook folder path. Use forward slashes for nested folders (e.g. Donor_Related/Grant). Folder is created if it doesn't exist.

AI classification (the prompt)

The plain-language description GPT reads to decide when an email belongs to this category. Be specific. Include example situations. If MLFA changes what constitutes a "donor inquiry," this is the field to edit — no code change needed.

Forwarding

Searchable list of recipients from the Recipients directory. Check the ones this category should auto-forward to. Empty means don't forward.

Reply

  • Default template — the auto-reply sent when this category matches. Pick from the Reply Templates library.
  • Personal reply template — used when GPT flags the email as needing a personal touch (case-specific facts, referrals). Falls back to the default if empty.
  • Amount threshold ($) — only forward when GPT detects a dollar amount at or above this. Used for donor and invoice rules. Leave blank if amount doesn't matter.

Behavior toggles

ToggleWhat it does

Send the auto-replyMaster switch. If off, no reply goes out even if a template is selected.
Reply only once per threadPrevents duplicate auto-replies when a sender follows up. Recommended for any category that replies.

Settings → Recipients

The staff directory. Categories can forward to any recipient here.

Fields

  • Label — human name (e.g. "Maria Laura").
  • Email — actual address (e.g. maria.laura@mlfa.org).
  • Notes — free-text purpose (e.g. "Violation notice routing").
  • Status — Active or Inactive. Inactive recipients can still be linked but won't receive forwards.

What happens when you edit

Changing a recipient's email address cascades — every category that forwards to them gets updated automatically. Same for deletion: the address is removed from every category's forwarding list.

Settings → Reply templates

Reusable auto-reply bodies that categories can point to.

Fields

  • Name — internal label (e.g. "Legal / Jail Mail default reply").
  • Message body — the actual text of the reply. Blank lines create paragraphs. URLs become clickable. Use {{greeting}} where you want an auto-generated greeting like "Dear Sarah,".
  • Active — inactive templates stay in the library but aren't selectable by new categories.

Preview button (eye icon)

On each template row. Opens a rendered preview showing what the reply will actually look like, with the greeting substituted with a sample name.

Editing a template

Changes cascade to every category using that template — you don't have to reselect anywhere.

Greeting variableType {{greeting}} anywhere in the template body. At send time it becomes "Dear FirstName," if GPT identified the sender's name, or "Good afternoon," if not.

Who can sign in to the hub, and what they can do. Admin only.

Fields per user

  • Display name — how the user appears in the audit log.
  • Role — admin, owner, or reviewer (see Roles & permissions above).
  • Assigned inboxes — for owners and reviewers only. Which mailboxes this user can access. Admins see all regardless.

Fields

PAIRActioned tags

Every email the hub touches gets an Outlook category (visible as a chip on the message in Outlook). This is how the worker knows what it already handled.

TagMeaning
PAIRActionedUmbrella marker. Always present on any email the hub touched. Used by the poller to skip already-processed messages.
PAIRActioned/<key>e.g. PAIRActioned/donor, PAIRActioned/legal. The category the AI assigned.
PAIRActioned/queuedThe email is in the review queue awaiting a person. Gets removed when Approved/Rejected/Dismissed.

Categories in Outlook are just labels — no colors are set by us. If you want them color-coded in Outlook, you can configure that on the mailbox side.

Edge cases & FAQs

Why do some emails stay in the Inbox with no tag?

A few reasons:

  • Sent by someone at info@mlfa.org — the worker only processes incoming mail. Emails sent from the mailbox itself stay untouched.
  • Sender is on the "Ignore senders" list — the worker silently skips these. No tag, no processing.
  • The worker was down when the email arrived, and the delta token hasn't been rebuilt yet. Rare.
  • The email was already tagged manually in Outlook before the hub was set up — but doesn't start with PAIRActioned. The hub only skips messages that start with that prefix.

Why do some emails have PAIRActioned/<key> but stay in the Inbox?

Because that category has no destination folder configured. The email got classified and tagged, but there's nowhere to move it. Common for categories like active_communication that are meant for humans to handle in-place. Fix: set a destination folder on the category if you want it moved.

Why does the AI classify a follow-up email differently than the original?

Because the AI sees the current message and (optionally) thread context — not always the full history. If the earlier messages in the thread went to a different address (not info@mlfa.org), the hub never saw them, so the classifier only has the current short follow-up to go on. This is expected behavior; tune the classification prompts to catch short follow-ups that reference the subject line.

Rachel replied to an email — does the hub know?

No. The hub only sees incoming messages. Rachel's outgoing reply from her @mlfa.org address doesn't come back through the worker. The "Reply Completed" badge you might see in Outlook is Outlook's own feature, not the hub.

Why did the same donor email get tagged both donor and grant?

Because GPT decided the email matched both category prompts. Categories aren't exclusive. If the donor prompt says "donations" and the grant prompt says "matching-gift programs, PayPal Giving Fund" and the email mentions both, both apply. Multiple tags means multiple actions can trigger (forward to give@ AND to grants team, for example).

What happens if GPT returns an unknown category?

Router forces the email into the review queue as a safety guardrail — even if automation is on. This prevents auto-processing something the system doesn't have a plan for.

Can I make the AI learn from my rejections?

Not yet. Rejection reasons are logged in the audit log but not currently fed back into the classifier. This is on the roadmap (RAG / memory of past corrections) but not built.

What if two staff members open the same queued email at the same time?

Whoever clicks Approve/Reject/Dismiss first wins. The other person will see it disappear from their queue on the next auto-refresh.

Do rejected emails come back?

Rejected emails are moved to Outlook's Deleted Items folder and tagged so the worker skips them on future polls. They won't be re-processed unless someone manually removes the tag and moves them back.

Troubleshooting

An email should have matched a category but didn't

  1. Go to Settings → Test & preview.
  2. Paste the exact subject, sender, and body.
  3. Run the test and see what GPT returned.
  4. If it returned nothing — tighten the classification prompt on the target category with a positive example.
  5. If it returned the wrong category — tighten that category's prompt with a negative example ("Do NOT classify as X if...").

An auto-reply didn't send

  • Check the category has Send the auto-reply toggled on.
  • Check the category has a Reply template selected (not "Don't send an auto-reply").
  • Check the template's Active box is checked.
  • Check the Reply safeguard — if the thread already got a reply, subsequent messages in the same thread won't get another one.

A forward didn't go to the right person

  • Open the category, check the Forward list — the person's checkbox should be ticked.
  • If they were checked but no email went — check the recipient's Active flag in Settings → Recipients. Inactive recipients are excluded.
  • For donor/invoice: check the Amount threshold. If set, the forward only happens when GPT detected a dollar amount at or above that number.

Automation is on but emails still go to the queue

  • Check that automation is actually saved as on in Settings → Inboxes → Edit. The Hub toggle sometimes reflects state before a save completes.
  • If yes, the queued emails are the "unknown category" safety guardrail — GPT returned something the router doesn't recognize. Test them in Test & Preview to see what GPT is returning.

The Hub says "Session expired"

Sessions time out after 30 minutes of inactivity or 8 hours absolute. Sign in again.

I can't see one of my inboxes

You're either not assigned to it (ask an admin) or your role is Reviewer with restricted access. Admins see all inboxes automatically; owners and reviewers see only their assigned ones.
